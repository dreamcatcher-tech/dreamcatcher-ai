---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Core observation</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>This is not about calming parents down.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">It is about giving their energy a safe job.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>The surprising turn</h3>
      <p>Parental pressure is not noise. It is a resource with no interface yet: anxiety, experience, money, time, judgement, scenarios, and willingness to test.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">The risk if ignored</h3>
      <p>If the only place for that energy is conversation, it becomes repeated reassurance, side-channel pressure, and abstract debate.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Bad default</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Conversation-only parent engagement</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Parents ask for certainty the school cannot honestly provide yet.</li>
        <li>The school repeats caution without producing enough evidence.</li>
        <li>Useful offers of help disappear into inboxes.</li>
        <li>Every delay sounds like vagueness instead of a named blocker.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>No object, no progress.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">A meeting cannot absorb parental energy. A testable system can.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Better default</div>
  <h2>Make parents the first adult test population.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Adults can consent</h3><p>They can use rough systems, report discomfort, and tolerate iteration in a way children should not be asked to.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Adults can compare</h3><p>They know what current family, school, email, portal, and calendar workflows feel like.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Adults can fund or fix</h3><p>When a blocker is budget, time, domain expertise, or scenario coverage, parents can sometimes directly help.</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="dc-muted" style="font-size: .48em; margin-top: 28px;">This is not child exposure by another name. It is adult-first evidence gathering while child-facing gates remain closed.</p>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Surprising solution</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Give parents their own cores.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Not a parent dashboard. A real enduring core they can live with.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Why this changes the conversation</h3>
      <p>A parent who has used a core understands custody, memory, permissions, annoyance, cost, delight, and trust failure from the inside.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">What it produces</h3>
      <div class="dc-pill-row"><span class="dc-pill">adult usage evidence</span><span class="dc-pill">guardian test cases</span><span class="dc-pill">cost signals</span><span class="dc-pill">trust objections</span></div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">First port of call</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Replace the vague inbox with an agentic intake</h3>
      <p>A parent concern, offer, scenario, objection, funding idea, or policy question enters the problem register and is classified.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">The point</h3>
      <p>Parents get a concrete place to push. The school gets structured work instead of conversational churn.</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>“Talk to the agent first.”</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">The first useful parent-facing AI may be the one that absorbs parent energy.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Guardian tests</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Let parents try to distrust it.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Trust is earned by giving skeptical people a safe test harness.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Example guardian tests</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Can the Conscience say no and explain why?</li>
        <li>Can a parent approve a high-consequence action without owning the child’s private core?</li>
        <li>Can visibility be tuned between “nothing” and “everything”?</li>
        <li>Can export, deletion, restore, and emergency keys be exercised?</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">The bite object</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Show them the simulation</h3>
      <p>Parents and teachers should not only hear the roadmap. They should see a school-shaped sandbox, synthetic children, policy friction, dashboards, and what the Conscience blocks.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Why it works</h3>
      <p>A simulation turns abstract caution into a shared object: “what would you want to know here?” “what would make this feel too visible?” “what would make this feel safe enough?”</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>Give them something to interrogate.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">A concrete model is more useful than another conversation about AI in general.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Dashboard co-design</div>
  <h2>Parents can help decide what oversight should see.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Too little</h3><p>The school and parents see nothing useful until harm has already happened.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>The middle</h3><p>Permitted summaries, trend signals, explanations, and staged escalation under consent and policy.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Too much</h3><p>Raw visibility becomes surveillance, destroying the trust that makes the core valuable.</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="dc-muted" style="font-size: .48em; margin-top: 28px;">Parent participation here is taste tuning and governance tuning, not a substitute for child consent.</p>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Resource lanes</div>
  <h2>Every blocker should become a specific parent ask.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Time</h3><p>Scenario writing, testing sessions, dashboard review, field-note editing, parent interviews.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Expertise</h3><p>Learning support, law, cybersecurity, product design, governance, accounting, family logistics.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Funding</h3><p>Model credits, hosting, support load, evaluation work, dashboards, security review.</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="dc-muted" style="font-size: .48em; margin-top: 28px;">Do not ask for generic support. Ask for the exact thing blocking the next measured step.</p>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Roadmap posture</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>A roadmap is not a promise. It is a measuring surface.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Click each step and see why it is ahead, blocked, or waiting.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Useful roadmap statuses</h3>
      <div class="dc-pill-row"><span class="dc-pill">ready</span><span class="dc-pill">testing</span><span class="dc-pill">blocked by safety</span><span class="dc-pill">blocked by budget</span><span class="dc-pill">blocked by time</span><span class="dc-pill">waiting on decision</span></div>
      <p style="margin-top: 22px;">The parent-facing version should make progress inspectable without implying certainty where none exists.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Co-steering</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Parent cores should influence — not own — child cores</h3>
      <p>A parent core can offer family values, lessons, constraints, and permitted summaries through policy. The child core remains protected, attributed, and age-appropriately independent.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">The open tuning question</h3>
      <p>How much came from parent, school, child, collective wisdom, source material, or model? Influence attribution should be visible and adjustable.</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>The family becomes a governed wisdom stream.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Not one pot. Not surveillance. Permissioned influence among enduring cores.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Monthly proof</div>
  <h2>The parent update should show evidence, not reassurance.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>What passed</h3><p>Guardian tests, Conscience scorecards, recovery drills, simulation scenarios, cost checks.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>What failed</h3><p>Blocks that were too dumb, dashboards that were too invasive, costs that exceeded expectation.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>What changed</h3><p>Policy edits, visibility tuning, new asks, abandoned ideas, next cohort size.</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="dc-muted" style="font-size: .48em; margin-top: 28px;">Suggested parent-readable rhythm: Kristin AI Safety Field Notes.</p>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">First 30 days</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>1 · Five parent cores</h3><p>Kristin-address gated pilot slots. Adults use cores for real adult work and feedback.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>2 · Guardian test v1</h3><p>Run consent, visibility, recovery, cost, and Conscience drills; publish what failed.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>3 · Simulation preview</h3><p>Give parents and teachers the first school-shaped sandbox to interrogate.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 20px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>4 · Problem register</h3><p>Route every concern and offer into the pipeline.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>5 · Cost sheet</h3><p>Name model, hosting, support, and dashboard costs.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>6 · Field note</h3><p>Short public-safe update: evidence, blockers, asks, next safe step.</p></div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Closing claim</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Harnessing parents means giving them leverage, not control.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Make them testers, witnesses, contributors, and co-steerers of the safe path.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>The ask</h3>
      <p>Start with adult parent cores, guardian tests, a simulation surface, and a public-safe proof-point rhythm. Let parental energy help build the evidence needed before real students enter the system.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
