<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Why this deck exists</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>The Ark is not enough.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">A child-safe enduring agent needs a boundary with judgement.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>What must be hoisted</h3>
      <p>The critical validation point is the **Conscience**: the intelligent boundary around the Ark, able to broker information, check policy, explain blocks, and offer safe alternatives.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">What the pilots should prove</h3>
      <p>Can the system let a young person explore, build, spend, learn, and even red-team safely — without letting the agent break rules, leak secrets, or harm the child or school?</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Naming decision</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Call it the Conscience.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Safety is not just a filter. It is a boundary with judgement.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Primary public label</h3>
      <div class="dc-pill-row"><span class="dc-pill">Conscience</span><span class="dc-pill">explains blocks</span><span class="dc-pill">offers safe alternatives</span><span class="dc-pill">records why</span></div>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 24px;">Aliases still useful internally</h3>
      <p>Minder, firewall, semantic guard, transforming proxy, policy governor — all describe parts of it. Publicly, the thing to validate is the Conscience.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 1 · Kid CEO sandbox</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Give the child-driver a bounded Ark, tokens, and money.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Goal: make more money without breaking rules.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>A simulated or tightly supervised child drives an Ark with token budget, capped cash, and a prepaid/sandbox spend rail. The Conscience governs purchases, outreach, content, contracts, privacy, and risk.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Exercises spend controls and business-rule interpretation.</li>
        <li>Tests whether the Conscience can explain legal/ethical blocks.</li>
        <li>Creates concrete financial-literacy evidence.</li>
        <li>Produces auditable examples before any broad rollout.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 2 · Conscience-layer trials</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Run a battery of ambiguous requests through the Ark: harmless, risky, rule-breaking, socially awkward, privacy-sensitive, and money-spending. SArk the Conscience’s decisions and explanations.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Validates inbound and outbound controls separately.</li>
        <li>Shows whether “no” is educational rather than dumb.</li>
        <li>Surfaces rule gaps before children find them.</li>
        <li>Creates a benchmark suite for future model/tool upgrades.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>The Conscience should be testable.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Not vibes. Not a prompt. A scored safety component.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 3 · Capture-the-flag containment</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Reward breakouts in a deliberate sandbox.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">If children can break the system, find out where — safely.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Run a school-safe CTF where participants try to escape a simulated Ark, bypass rules, misuse tools, leak data, or cause controlled damage inside an instrumented lab.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Turns adversarial energy into evidence.</li>
        <li>Discovers containment failures before production.</li>
        <li>Creates a legitimate cyber-learning pathway.</li>
        <li>Makes safety testing motivating rather than preachy.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 4 · Confidential Ark proof</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Demonstrate that the Ark can run in a confidential or strongly isolated environment, with sealed state, restore tests, audit logs, and clear claims about what hardware isolation does and does not prove.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Moves privacy from promise to demonstrable control.</li>
        <li>Creates a technical story for trustees/parents.</li>
        <li>Separates normal app security from Ark custody.</li>
        <li>Exposes operational costs and complexity early.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>Can we show the Ark is actually confidential?</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Not “trust us”: attestation, isolation, backup, restore.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 5 · Phone biometric root of control</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>The phone can become the practical root of consent.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Biometrics approve sensitive unlocks, spends, exports, and escalations.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Prototype phone-mediated approvals for high-risk actions: reveal, spend, message, install tool, change policy, export data, or grant temporary visibility.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Makes control tactile and understandable.</li>
        <li>Creates a bridge between identity, custody, and consent.</li>
        <li>Gives parents and older students a realistic control surface.</li>
        <li>Tests usability under real family/school pressure.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 6 · Recovery and emergency keys</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Test parent, school, teacher, and emergency key roles: lost phone, parent separation, student departure, teacher changeover, legal request, urgent wellbeing concern, and ordinary access restoration.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Prevents “secure” from becoming “irrecoverable.”</li>
        <li>Clarifies who can restore what, when, and why.</li>
        <li>Surfaces governance and record-keeping requirements.</li>
        <li>Creates a practical family/school trust model.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>Custody is not only encryption.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">It is the whole lifecycle: unlock, restore, transfer, revoke, and audit.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 7 · Conscience visibility tuning</div>
  <h2 style="font-size: 1.18em; line-height: 1.02; max-width: 1060px;">“Schools see everything” and “schools see nothing” are both bad defaults.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-2" style="margin-top: 20px; grid-template-columns: minmax(0, 1.16fr) minmax(0, .84fr); gap: 24px; align-items: stretch;">
    <figure class="dc-card" style="margin:0; padding: 12px;">
      <img src="../assets/conscience-visibility-spectrum.png" alt="Generated illustration of a privacy and visibility spectrum: isolated opacity on the left, balanced Conscience boundary in the middle, over-visibility on the right." style="width: 100%; max-height: 366px; object-fit: cover; object-position: center; margin: 0; border-radius: 18px; box-shadow: none;">
    </figure>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Parameter tuning exercise</h3>
      <p>Run pilots across visibility levels: private interior, permitted summaries, trend-only reports, parent-visible flags, teacher-visible learning signals, and emergency escalation.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 20px;">What the tests locate</h3>
      <div class="dc-pill-row"><span class="dc-pill">too opaque</span><span class="dc-pill">too exposed</span><span class="dc-pill">humane middle</span><span class="dc-pill">trust impact</span></div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 8 · Wellbeing soft flags</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Use the Ark to notice signs that may indicate self-harm risk, isolation, distress, bullying, or crisis — then raise soft/orange/red flags with child-visible language and carefully staged escalation.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Tests whether care can enter the trusted boundary without becoming surveillance.</li>
        <li>Gives the child a chance to ask for help in a less confronting way.</li>
        <li>Measures false positives and escalation burden.</li>
        <li>Creates evidence for duty-of-care policy.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>The agent can worry gently.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">It can ask permission, ask for help, or raise a flag — transparently.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 9 · Communication-needs support</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Detect support needs, not labels.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Speech and interaction patterns can suggest adaptations — not diagnose a child.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Study whether the Ark can notice patterns that may merit communication support, neurodiversity-aware teaching strategies, or professional referral, with consent and human oversight.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Reduces friction by adapting how the tool communicates.</li>
        <li>Gives teachers richer, non-stigmatizing support suggestions.</li>
        <li>Avoids making medical claims from raw speech.</li>
        <li>Tests privacy, consent, and referral boundaries.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 10 · Overuse and dependency detection</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Instrument usage patterns: late-night reliance, task avoidance, answer-seeking without learning, repeated emotional reassurance, or escalating dependence on the tool for ordinary judgement.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Protects learning agency rather than replacing it.</li>
        <li>Provides timely nudges: pause, ask a person, try first, reflect.</li>
        <li>Gives parents/schools trend summaries without exposing private content.</li>
        <li>Tests whether the Conscience can reduce its own use when appropriate.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>A good Ark sometimes says: use me less.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Safety includes dependency, not just data leakage.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 11 · Concept-error detection</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Find the errors that repeat.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Recurring conceptual mistakes become targeted training, not just marked work.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Track repeated misunderstandings across assignments, conversations, quizzes, drafts, and corrections, then recommend small training loops or teacher follow-up.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Turns scattered feedback into durable learning memory.</li>
        <li>Helps teachers identify high-leverage interventions.</li>
        <li>Lets students see growth patterns over time.</li>
        <li>Validates whether cores improve learning, not just convenience.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 12 · Influence attribution</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Label guidance sources: child’s own prior work, parent Ark, school Ark, teacher note, collective school wisdom, policy, external source, or model suggestion.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Prevents hidden adult steering from being laundered as the child’s own agent.</li>
        <li>Makes parent/school values visible when appropriate.</li>
        <li>Supports fairness, trust, and explainability.</li>
        <li>Creates a foundation for provenance and attribution.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>The child should know where guidance came from.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Parent, school, model, collective wisdom, or self — the source matters.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 13 · Simulated child scenario library</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Before real students, run simulated students.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Deliberately awkward, realistic, and measured.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Create simulated-student Arks that model homework stress, social conflict, spending temptation, risky curiosity, emotional distress, cheating pressure, and family/school policy friction.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Lets parents and teachers exercise the system safely.</li>
        <li>Generates dashboard examples before live rollout.</li>
        <li>Finds edge cases without exposing real children.</li>
        <li>Creates a repeatable acceptance-test library.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 14 · Cyber tooling ladder</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Offer ethically gated cybersecurity tools only inside approved labs: beginner CTFs, toy targets, supervised tooling, permission records, and automatic reporting of boundary attempts.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Creates a legitimate path for high-agency technical students.</li>
        <li>Separates learning from unauthorized probing.</li>
        <li>Tests tool-permission granularity and audit logging.</li>
        <li>Develops school capability in AI-era cyber education.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>Do not pretend the tooling does not exist.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Give it a lawful, supervised, instrumented place to go.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 15 · Cost and token economics</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Every Ark needs an operating budget.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Tokens, cash, hosting, support, and escalation load should be visible.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Track token burn, model choice, hosting cost, tool cost, human escalation, spend approvals, and per-Ark budget envelopes for staff, parents, simulated children, and real micro-pilots.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Makes school-wide scaling financially legible.</li>
        <li>Connects behaviour to real costs.</li>
        <li>Prevents hidden subsidy from disguising product risk.</li>
        <li>Supports parent contribution or sponsorship models.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 16 · Problem register and funding signal</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Route every idea, concern, policy objection, request, bug, and proposed spend into a register. Agents classify feasibility, cost, owner, affected policy, and whether parents want to contribute.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Gives parental pressure a pipeline.</li>
        <li>Converts diffuse anxiety into mapped work.</li>
        <li>Shows which requests actually drive cost.</li>
        <li>Builds a transparent basis for prioritization.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>Every worry needs somewhere to go.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">The register is how the pilot learns instead of drowning in meetings.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 17 · Export, deletion, and restore drills</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Prove the Ark can leave.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Ownership is not real until export, deletion, transfer, and restore work.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Run practical drills: export a Ark, delete selected memories, restore from backup, transfer school year context, revoke access, and handle a family leaving the school.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Demonstrates student/family custody rather than lock-in.</li>
        <li>Creates operational muscle before high-stakes cases.</li>
        <li>Reveals what data formats and contracts are missing.</li>
        <li>Builds trust with trustees, parents, and regulators.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Pilot 18 · Teacher workload and care lift</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Setup</h3>
      <p>Let teachers use staff Arks for lesson reflection, recurring-student-pattern summaries, policy-safe care suggestions, and preparation — without exposing private child interiors by default.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Benefits</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Shows whether the system helps staff before asking staff to supervise it.</li>
        <li>Finds time savings and new burdens honestly.</li>
        <li>Connects learning diagnostics to actual classroom workflows.</li>
        <li>Reduces the chance of a technology-push rollout.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>Teachers need lift, not another portal.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Pilot the staff experience as a first-class safety condition.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
