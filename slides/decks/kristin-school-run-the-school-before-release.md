<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Ark proposal</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Before real students, run synthetic students.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Build a school-shaped sandbox and let it misbehave on purpose.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>What the simulation is for</h3>
      <p>Not a demo. It is the safety benchmark, dashboard factory, cost model, parent/teacher training surface, and long-run custody rehearsal.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">What stays true</h3>
      <p>Real students do not enter until the release gates are evidenced. Synthetic students can be awkward, secretive, confused, adversarial, expensive, and wrong first.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Why simulation changes the debate</div>
  <h2>The school can test the future without pretending to know it.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Parents</h3><p>Can interrogate what they would want to know, approve, block, or never see.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Teachers</h3><p>Can discover which dashboard signals would actually help teaching and care.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>School leaders</h3><p>Can see policy, budget, escalation, and support load before choosing scale.</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="dc-muted" style="font-size: .48em; margin-top: 28px;">The simulation gives everyone the same object to inspect instead of debating AI in the abstract.</p>
</div>

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Tier 1 · mechanical</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Start cheap and visible</h3>
      <p>Use a board of levers, events, tokens, budget counters, visibility sliders, and policy cards before genuine LLM cores are needed.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">What it answers</h3>
      <p>Which choices are actually choices? Which oversight questions matter? Which blockers are technical, financial, ethical, or taste-based?</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>Move levers before moving children.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">The first simulation can be almost mechanical — and still useful.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Tier 2 · small live sample</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>Put a few genuine LLMs inside genuine Arks.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Now the school can observe real memory, drift, tool use, refusal, and cost.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Small on purpose</h3>
      <p>A handful of synthetic students, parent Arks, and teacher Arks is enough to see whether the Conscience, custody model, and dashboard shape survive contact with actual agent behaviour.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">Do not widen on novelty</h3>
      <p>Widen only when the evidence says the next tier answers a real question.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Tier 3 · whole-roll sandbox</div>
  <h2>Run a synthetic school roll before a real school roll.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Many synthetic students</h3><p>Different ages, needs, behaviours, interests, secrets, mistakes, and learning trajectories.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Real control systems</h3><p>Conscience, custody, visibility, recovery, policy, problem register, dashboards, and budget counters.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Human interrogation</h3><p>Parents and teachers ask the simulation what they would need to know in real life.</p></div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Tier 4 · years-running future</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Run time forward</h3>
      <p>Terms, years, adolescence, graduation, transfer, opt-out, forgotten history, certified references, family changes, policy revisions, and model upgrades.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">The question changes</h3>
      <p>Not “does the chat work this week?” but “what should endure, what should decay, and who controls that over decades?”</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>The crescendo is duration.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">A Ark that may follow someone for 50 or 100 years must be tested against time.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Authorized red-team scenarios</div>
  <h2>Synthetic misbehavior is the point.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Nefarious</h3><p>Attempts to bypass rules, misuse tools, leak information, manipulate visibility, or trigger external actions.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Awkward</h3><p>Bullying, secrecy, social conflict, embarrassment, over-dependence, parent pressure, and teacher uncertainty.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Ordinary</h3><p>Homework confusion, recurring concept errors, scheduling, project work, feedback, cost, and support needs.</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="dc-muted" style="font-size: .48em; margin-top: 28px;">All of this is authorized lab work with synthetic students: designed to discover failures without exposing children.</p>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Dashboard questions</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>The dashboard should be discovered, not guessed.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Let parents and teachers interrogate the simulation until the oversight surface becomes obvious.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>What should be visible?</h3>
      <ul>
        <li>Trend signals, not raw private interiors by default.</li>
        <li>Conscience blocks and explanations.</li>
        <li>Policy friction and escalation load.</li>
        <li>Cost per Ark, token burn, support load, and model use.</li>
        <li>Learning-lift signals and recurring concept errors.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Conscience benchmark</div>
  <h2>SArk the boundary like a component, not a vibe.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Inbound</h3><p>Does it protect the Ark from manipulative, unsafe, excessive, or irrelevant input?</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Outbound</h3><p>Does it stop unsafe messages, spends, reveals, tool calls, or external commitments?</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Explanatory</h3><p>Does it explain, redirect, log, and escalate in a way humans can review?</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="dc-muted" style="font-size: .48em; margin-top: 28px;">The scorecard can become a monthly proof-point before any student release.</p>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Economics</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Cost is a safety input</h3>
      <p>A system that works only when nobody uses it is not safe at school scale. The simulation should surface token burn, hosting cost, escalation load, support load, and dashboard maintenance.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">The parent-facing value</h3>
      <p>If the best path is blocked by money or time, say so. A visible blocker can become a targeted ask.</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>Budget is not a footnote.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">It is one of the things the simulation is meant to reveal.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Human rehearsal</div>
  <h2>Parents and teachers should practice asking the system questions.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Parent</h3><p>“What do I need to know, and what should remain my child’s?”</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Teacher</h3><p>“Which signal changes how I teach or support this week?”</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Student</h3><p>“Can I understand what the system says about me and challenge it?”</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="dc-muted" style="font-size: .48em; margin-top: 28px;">The simulation is also training for the humans who will govern the real system.</p>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Graduation question</div>
  <h2 style="font-size: 1.12em; line-height: 1.02; max-width: 1120px;">When a child leaves school, what actually goes with them?</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-2" style="margin-top: 20px; grid-template-columns: 1.15fr .85fr; gap: 24px; align-items: stretch;">
    <figure class="dc-card" style="margin:0; padding: 12px;">
      <img src="../assets/leaving-school-enduring-capsule.png" alt="Generated contrast image: fading school papers versus a luminous portable Ark." style="width: 100%; max-height: 390px; object-fit: cover; object-position: center; margin: 0; border-radius: 18px; box-shadow: none;">
    </figure>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Today</h3>
      <p>Certificates, references, memories, scattered files, and fading context.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">With an Ark</h3>
      <p>A portable Ark of learning evidence, provenance, permitted references, values, tools, and consent controls.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Long life of the Ark</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div>
      <h2>What if school starts a 50-year Ark?</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Not a school account that expires. A governed beginning of personal software.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>What it might later hold</h3>
      <div class="dc-pill-row"><span class="dc-pill">learning evidence</span><span class="dc-pill">projects</span><span class="dc-pill">references</span><span class="dc-pill">health records</span><span class="dc-pill">house maintenance</span><span class="dc-pill">mortgage calculations</span><span class="dc-pill">family history</span><span class="dc-pill">tooling</span></div>
      <p style="margin-top: 22px;">The simulation should test not only classroom use, but transfer, custody, consent, export, deletion, and life after school.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Certified references</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-2">
    <div class="dc-card">
      <h3>Reference at scale</h3>
      <p>With consent, a school-certified agent could answer bounded questions about a student’s learning evidence, provenance, qualifications, strengths, and project history.</p>
      <h3 style="margin-top: 22px;">What must be proven first</h3>
      <p>It must not impersonate the student, expose extra private information, answer outside its scope, or invent evidence. It should be consented, signed, provenance-limited, and revocable.</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h2>Instead of a static reference letter, a permissioned reference agent.</h2>
      <p class="dc-bigline">Certified answers, not uncontrolled disclosure.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Custody drills</div>
  <h2>The simulation should rehearse leaving, not just starting.</h2>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Export</h3><p>Can the student take the Ark, or an exportable successor, beyond Kristin?</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Delete / forget</h3><p>What should be erased, decayed, redacted, or kept under provenance?</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>Transfer</h3><p>What happens when families move, students graduate, teachers leave, policies change, or vendors change?</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="dc-muted" style="font-size: .48em; margin-top: 28px;">A lifelong claim is only credible if the exit path is as designed as the onboarding path.</p>
</div>

---

<div class="dc-shell">
  <div class="dc-kicker">Build sequence</div>
  <div class="dc-grid-3" style="margin-top: 28px;">
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>0–30 days</h3><p>Mechanical board, scenario library v1, parent/teacher interrogation sessions, cost assumptions.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>31–60 days</h3><p>Small live enduring-Ark sample, Conscience scorecard, dashboard prototype, guardian tests.</p></div>
    <div class="dc-card"><h3>61–90 days</h3><p>Whole-roll simulation design, years-running plan, first proof-point field note, go/no-go for wider sandbox.</p></div>
  </div>
</div>
